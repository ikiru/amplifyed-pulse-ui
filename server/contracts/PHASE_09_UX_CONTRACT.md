# 📘 AmplifyEd — Phase 9 UX Decisions (LOCKED)

**Phase:** 9
**Theme:** Persistence, Replay & Memory Without Surveillance
**Scope:** UX posture and meaning only
**Status:** COMPLETE

Phase 9 defines how AmplifyEd remembers a session **without converting memory into surveillance, evaluation, or retroactive pressure**.

Phase 8 is closed and binding. Nothing in this document reopens live-session behavior.

---

## Core Principle

> **Persistence must never create a second performance.**

Memory that changes how people behave later is surveillance, not learning.

---

## Phase 9.1 — Audience Memory Posture

### Locked Posture

After a session ends, an audience member should reasonably believe:

* Their silence was not noted
* Their participation was not scored
* Their timing was not tracked
* Their absence was not recorded
* Nothing they did (or didn’t do) will be replayed back to them later

This belief must hold even if the system retains room-level abstractions.

### Invariant

> **Audience memory must be non-attributable, non-replayable, and non-evaluative.**

Memory exists only if it cannot point back.

---

## Phase 9.2 — Trainer Replay Posture

### Purpose of Replay

Replay exists to support:

* trainer self-reflection
* facilitation skill growth
* curiosity without certainty

Replay does **not** exist to:

* establish truth
* justify decisions
* evaluate participants
* explain outcomes

### Required Felt Experience

When replay is viewed, it should feel:

* partial
* abstracted
* incomplete
* humility-inducing

Replay should **lower certainty**, not raise it.

### Authority Boundary

> **Replay may inform facilitation learning, but must never re-author the session’s meaning.**

---

## Phase 9.3 — Non-Persistable List (Hard Prohibitions)

The following must never persist in any form:

1. Individual messages (raw, cleaned, or summarized)
2. Message ordering or conversational sequence
3. Individual pulse interactions or histories
4. Join / leave / re-entry timing
5. Engagement inference (high/low participation)
6. Individual presence or absence
7. Emotional labels applied to people
8. Safety exception content or indicators
9. Trainer–audience attribution narratives
10. Comparative session memory (improvement / regression)

### Meta-Rule

> **If a reasonable person could recognize themselves in replay, it must not persist.**

This rule overrides clever abstraction.

---

## Phase 9.4 — Safety Exception Memory Posture

### Total Containment

A safety exception must terminate memory, not transform it.

After containment:

* No replay shows it
* No aggregate hints at it
* No gaps imply it
* No summaries reference it
* No future cues are influenced by it

### Trainer Perspective

* Trainer may know containment occurred in the moment
* Trainer cannot revisit, reference, or prove it later

### Hard Constraint

> **No role should later be able to tell whether a safety exception occurred at all.**

Safety that leaves residue is not safety.

---

## Phase 9 — Global Invariants

Across all persistence and replay surfaces:

* Silence remains valid across time
* Absence remains non-legible
* Memory does not imply accountability
* Replay does not create authority
* Safety overrides visibility
* Meaning belongs to the room, not hindsight
